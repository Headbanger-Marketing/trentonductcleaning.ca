Describing the outside hoods, caps and screens where vents end

The outside end of a vent may show what the inside cannot. First list each termination you can see from the ground at your Trenton home, then note where each one is, and last say whether it looks clear. A flap that sits shut, a screen packed with lint or a hood partly hidden by snow or plants may point to a restriction further along the run.

  1. Return grilles

    Where room air is drawn back toward the furnace.

  2. Return ducts

    The runs leading from each grille to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    The blower, evaporator coil and cabinet.

  4. Supply trunk

    The largest duct carrying air away from the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    The individual runs that split off toward each room.

  6. Supply registers

    The floor, wall or ceiling vents in each room.

Separate runs to ask about:Dryer ventHRV core and screensBathroom fan ductsRange hood exhaust

  • Where each vent ends

    Note which wall each hood or cap sits on, and roughly how high above the ground it is.

  • What you see there

    Describe any flap, screen or louvre, and whether it looks open, stuck or covered by lint, snow, a nest or plants.

  • Photos from a safe spot

    Take wide and close photos from the ground, standing well back from any ladder, roof edge or nest.

Questions About Your Trenton Request

Does Trenton Duct Cleaning send its own crew to clean ducts and vents?

Trenton Duct Cleaning cannot clean anything itself and has no office, crew or vehicles. It is an enquiry website for Trenton, owned by a marketing company, and not a contractor. Only a responding provider you agree with does the work, and before anything is arranged that provider must identify themselves and confirm what they cover. Nothing here is a quote.

Is a visit or a price agreed when I send a Trenton request?

You still need to agree the pricing, the written scope, the timing and the provider directly before any work starts. Sending a request means our enquiry team reviews it and may pass it to a provider who identifies themselves and confirms they cover your address. Nothing is booked or priced at that stage, and no result is promised.

Can I send a Trenton duct cleaning request if I only have photos of the outside hoods?

Yes. Photos of each hood or cap from the ground, with a note on where it sits and how high, give our enquiry team and any responding provider a starting point. You do not need to know which system each opening serves; say what you cannot identify and ask the provider to confirm it before any scope is agreed.

What should I photograph at my Trenton home before sending a request?

Photograph each outside hood, cap and screen you can see from the ground, plus a wider shot showing the wall around it. Add a photo of the label on the furnace, air handler or HRV if you can reach it safely. Keep your feet on the ground and stay clear of ladders, roof edges and anything that may hold a nest, and send what you have; a partial set still helps.

What if a bird or animal nest is blocking an outside vent at my Trenton house?

Leave it alone. Do not reach up, climb or poke at it, and do not go on a roof. Photograph the hood from a safe spot on the ground, note its height, and mention the nest in your request so the provider can say how it would be handled and whether a separate visit is needed.

Should I clear snow or plants away from a hood before the provider looks?

Clearing loose snow or cutting back plants at ground level is ordinary yard care, but photograph the hood first so the provider sees how it looked. Do not use tools on the hood itself, open it or pull anything from inside it. If it is high, icy or hard to reach, leave it and describe it instead, and the provider can say how it would be reached.

What do I say if a vent hood outside looks fine but the dryer still struggles?

Describe both: the clean-looking hood and what the dryer does, such as clothes staying damp or the flap not moving when it runs. A hood that looks clear does not show the state of the whole run behind it, so ask the provider how the route would be assessed and which sections can be reached. A photo of the connector behind the dryer, taken without moving the dryer, also helps.

Who decides what happens if the outside caps at my Trenton building are shared?

The person who can authorize work decides, and that may be a landlord, a property manager or a condominium corporation rather than you. Name them in your request and say who pays and who can give access. Ask them directly about any lease or building rule before work is agreed; this site cannot say what applies to your building.
